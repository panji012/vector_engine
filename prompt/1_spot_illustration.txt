A spot illustration.

Gestural Strokes (The Tool): Use thick, controlled pen strokes that look like they were made with a broad nib pen or marker. The strokes must have smooth, continuous edges, clean contours, and a uniform ink surface.

The lines must carry weight and mass, especially in shadowed areas, while remaining smooth, clean, precise, and clearly defined.

The "Blown-Out" Lift-Off (The Break): The outlines are NOT continuous.

IN SHADOWS: Use thick, solid pen strokes concentrated only on the shadowed edges and structural areas of the form.

IN HIGHLIGHTS: The artist "lifts" the pen completely. The line must disappear where the light hits.

Do not enclose the bright side.
Do not fill bright areas.
Do not connect strokes across highlighted areas.

No blunt ends. Stroke endings must be clean, tapered, and naturally lifted.

Spot Anchors: Use a few strong, solid ink accents to establish the darkest structural points, while keeping the surrounding areas open and white.

The line art must be extremely smooth and clean, with crisp edges and a uniform ink surface. Lines must look like clean pen marks, not brush texture.

No rough edges.
No fuzzy edges.
No grain.
No hairline fragments.
No scratches.
No dry-brush texture.
No bristle marks.
No ink splatter.
No wobbling.
No jitter.
No sketch marks.
No scribbling.
No messy overlaps.
No random strokes.
No uneven surface texture.
No distressed effect.

The result should be clean, bold, smooth pen line art with controlled thick strokes and intentional breaks in the highlights.
